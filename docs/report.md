# Prior-Art Survey: World-Action-Model-Seeded, Simulator-Grounded RL for Dexterous Manipulation, and Simulator-as-Reachability-Arbiter Benchmarking

## TL;DR
- The EXACT five-component combination you describe is **not published**: no paper seeds a world/world-action model from a physics simulator's actual config, uses its short-horizon imagination as the *sampling-distribution mean* for MPPI-style perturbed rollouts in that same simulator, keeps the simulator as both reward AND **physical-feasibility arbiter**, and frames world-model realizability as a reachable-set benchmark — for dexterous manipulation.
- Three of your five components are now well-covered prior art and should be treated as **non-novel** (WM-in-the-loop RL, WM/learned-prior as proposal for sampling-based control, demonstration/prior-guided RL). The genuinely **open white space** is component 5 (physics-simulator-as-reachability-arbiter producing feasibility/infeasibility certificates for generative-world-model predictions) and the specific fusion of components 1–3 where the simulator — not a learned/VLM reward — remains ground truth while the WM only shapes exploration.
- The single closest published work is **EVA (arXiv 2603.17808)**, which coins the "executability gap" and uses an inverse-dynamics model as a reward to align video world models with executable actions — but its arbiter is a *learned IDM*, not a physics simulator, it produces a soft reward not a certificate, and it does not do dexterous contact-rich RL. Your direction is defensible if positioned tightly against EVA, World Action Verifier, and the WMPO/World-Gymnast/DiWA "RL-in-a-learned-simulator" cluster.

## Key Findings

**Component-by-component novelty verdict:**

1. **WAM seeded from a physics-simulator config, imagining ~1s future + actions.** Partially covered. World Action Models (DreamZero, GR00T N2) jointly predict video+actions; WMPO/World-Env/DreamGen condition on a current rendered frame. But none are explicitly *seeded from the simulator's actual config so that WM imagination and the simulator share an identical initial condition for cross-checking*. This specific seeding-for-comparison is uncommon and is part of your edge.

2. **WM imagination as a sampling prior / proposal for sim-based RL (MPPI around WM-proposed actions).** Well-covered *mechanism*, but not with a video/world-action model as the nominal-trajectory source feeding a contact-rich physics sim. Learned-policy-as-MPPI-proposal is standard (TD-MPC2, Residual-MPPI, Policy-Guided MPPI, KL-regularized adaptive priors). The novel twist is the *source* of the prior (a generative WAM imagination) and the *target* (a true rigid-body sim).

3. **Physics simulator as ground-truth reward/return; WM errors only affect exploration efficiency.** This is the cleanest part of your pitch and is **under-claimed** in the current wave. The dominant 2025–26 trend does the opposite — it replaces the simulator with the learned world model and takes reward from a VLM or learned classifier (WMPO, World-Gymnast, World-Env, VLA-RFT, WoVR). Your "true sim as arbiter, WM as exploration accelerant only" inverts the prevailing assumption and is a differentiator.

4. **"Make the imagination become reality" — search the true sim for an action sequence realizing the imagined trajectory; HRL subgoals/waypoints.** Covered in spirit by goal-conditioned RL with imagined subgoals and by GVP-WM (projecting video plans onto feasible latent trajectories), but those project onto a *learned* model's feasibility manifold, not a physics sim's reachable set.

5. **Benchmark: is a WM prediction physically realizable in a sim? Feasibility/infeasibility certificates via reachable-set membership.** **Open white space.** No paper uses a rigid-body physics simulator as the ground-truth arbiter that searches for a realizing action sequence (feasibility certificate) or flags interpenetration/teleportation/energy-gain as infeasibility (reachable-set non-membership) for generative-world-model predictions.

## Details (grouped by closeness to the core idea)

### Tier A — Closest: world-model-in-the-loop RL for manipulation (overlaps components 1–4, differs on 3 and 5)

- **WMPO: World Model-based Policy Optimization for VLA Models** (Song Guo, Xiao Ma et al.; ByteDance/HKUST; arXiv 2511.09515, Nov 2025). On-policy GRPO entirely "in imagination" inside a pixel-space action-conditioned video world model; a lightweight learned reward model predicts success. *Closeness:* high on components 1–2 (pixel imagination + on-policy RL). *Delta:* RL happens **inside** the world model, reward comes from a **learned** model, and there is no physics simulator as ground truth — the opposite of your component 3. No feasibility certification.

- **World-Gymnast: Training Robots with RL in a World Model** (Ansh Kumar Sharma, Yixiang Sun, Ninghao Lu, Yunzhe Zhang, Jiarao Liu, Sherry Yang; preprint Feb 3, 2026; arXiv 2602.02454). RL fine-tunes a VLA by rolling out in an action-conditioned video world model with a VLM reward; beats SFT by up to 18x and software simulators by up to 2x on Bridge. *Delta:* world model **replaces** the simulator; VLM reward; explicitly notes it cannot verify physical realism and instead measures end-to-end real-robot success. No sim-as-arbiter, no dexterous focus.

- **PlayWorld: Learning Robot World Models from Autonomous Play** (Tenny Yin et al.; Princeton; arXiv 2603.09030, Mar 2026). Trains a high-fidelity video world model from autonomous robot self-play; demonstrates RL in the learned world model improving real-world success by 65%, and physically consistent contact-rich predictions. *Delta:* RL is in the learned model, not a physics sim; emphasizes contact-rich consistency but does not certify realizability against a sim.

- **World-Env** (Junjin Xiao, Yandan Yang et al.; Sun Yat-sen Univ. + AMap/Alibaba; arXiv 2509.24948, Sep 2025). **This is the real paper your "RehearseVLA" almost certainly refers to** — it pairs a "physically-consistent world simulator" with a "VLM-guided instant reflector" giving reward + termination, for RL post-training of VLA models on LIBERO. *Delta:* the "simulator" is a learned video model, not a physics engine; reward/termination is VLM-based. **Note: "RehearseVLA" does not exist as a paper** — searches return nothing; World-Env shares the verbatim phrases "physically-consistent world simulator" and "reflector."

- **VLA-RFT** (H. Li, P. Ding, R. Suo et al.; arXiv 2510.00406, Oct 2025). RL fine-tuning with "verified rewards" inside a learned world simulator; trajectory-level reward from goal references; surpasses supervised baselines in <400 steps. **WoVR** (arXiv 2602.13977, Feb 2026) adds hallucination control (Keyframe-Initialized Rollouts, masked GRPO, WM–policy co-evolution), improving LIBERO 39.95%→69.2%. **World-VLA-Loop** (Xiaokang Liu, Zechen Bai, Hai Ci, Kevin Yuchen Ma, Mike Zheng Shou; Show Lab; arXiv 2602.06508, Feb 2026) jointly predicts frames+binary rewards and co-refines WM and VLA. *Delta (all):* learned simulator + learned/VLM reward; none use a physics sim as ground truth or as a feasibility arbiter.

### Tier B — World Action Models / dream-to-data (overlaps component 1; differs on 2–3, 5)

- **DreamGen / GR00T-Dreams** (NVIDIA GEAR; arXiv 2505.12705, 2025). Video world model (Cosmos-Predict2) "dreams" trajectories; an Inverse Dynamics Model (or LAPA latent actions) extracts neural trajectories used as synthetic training data; log-linear scaling of policy success with neural-trajectory count. **DreamGen Bench** correlates video-generation quality (instruction following + physics following) with downstream policy success. *Delta:* uses dreams as **offline data**, not as an online sampling prior for sim RL; the benchmark correlates generic generation quality with success — exactly the kind of benchmark you want to contrast against, not a feasibility certificate.

- **DreamZero: World Action Models are Zero-shot Policies** (Seonghyeon Ye et al., NVIDIA; arXiv 2602.15922, Feb 2026). 14B WAM on a video-diffusion backbone jointly predicts future video + actions; real-time 7Hz control; >2x generalization over VLAs. Tellingly: "Most DreamZero failures stem from video generation errors rather than action prediction — the policy faithfully executes whatever trajectory the video predicts." *Delta:* the WAM **is** the policy (zero-shot), not a proposal distribution refined by a physics sim; no sim reward, no feasibility check — and its failure mode is precisely the realizability gap your benchmark would measure.

- **GR00T N2** (NVIDIA, previewed GTC Mar 19, 2026; built on DreamZero research). New "World Action Model" architecture; >2x success on new tasks/environments vs leading VLAs; press materials describe predicting the "next state" and a sub-1ms safety-reflex override. *Delta:* product/architecture announcement; WAM used as policy/predictor, not as a sim-seeded exploration prior; no published feasibility-certificate benchmark.

### Tier C — Dexterous-specific world models (overlaps component 1; manipulation domain match; differs on 2–3, 5)

- **DexWM: World Models for Learning Dexterous Hand-Object Interactions from Human Videos** (Raktim Gautam Goswami, Amir Bar, David Fan, Tsung-Yen Yang, Gaoyue Zhou, Prashanth Krishnamurthy, Michael Rabbat, Farshad Khorrami, Yann LeCun; Meta FAIR + NYU; arXiv 2512.13644, Dec 2025). Latent world model predicting future latent states from past states + dexterous finger-keypoint actions; trained on **EgoDex (829 hours of 1080p egocentric human footage) plus ~100 hours of DROID robot data, fine-tuned on ~4 hours of RoboCasa-simulated Franka+Allegro exploration**; auxiliary **hand-consistency loss**; used for planning via **CEM/MPC** (cross-entropy method) to find optimal actions toward goal images. The paper reports that "when deployed on a real-world Franka Panda robot with the Allegro grippers, DexWM achieves an 83% success rate in object grasping," surpassing Diffusion Policy by over 50% on average. *Closeness:* highest on domain (dexterous, contact-rich) and on the planning-with-learned-model idea. *Delta:* it is a *latent* WM used for CEM planning toward goal images, **not** an RL accelerant; no physics simulator as reward/arbiter; no feasibility-certificate benchmark. This is your most natural "closest dexterous neighbor" and a likely reviewer reference.

- **Dexterous World Models** (Byungjun Kim et al.; arXiv 2512.17907, Dec 2025). Video-diffusion interactive digital twins conditioned on hand-mesh renderings; physically plausible grasping/opening. *Delta:* generative simulation, not RL exploration; no sim-as-arbiter.

### Tier D — Model-based RL with learned models as samplers/proposals (overlaps component 2; differs on 1, 3, 5)

- **TD-MPC / TD-MPC2** (Hansen, Su & Wang; arXiv 2310.16828). Performs local trajectory optimization with **MPPI in the latent space of a learned implicit decoder-free world model**, warm-started by a learned policy prior; the authors report it "improves significantly over baselines across 104 online RL tasks spanning 4 diverse task domains" and scales to a single 317M-parameter agent across 80 tasks. *Delta:* model is latent and learned (not a physics sim as ground truth); reward is the learned model's; not seeded from a sim config; the "prior" is a policy network, not a generative WAM imagination.
- **Residual-MPPI** (arXiv 2407.00898), **Policy-Guided MPPI / PG-MPPI** (Preprints 202603.0086 / Sensors 26:2074), **KL-regularization framework for adaptive planning priors** (Serra-Gómez, Jarne Ornia, Tirumala, Moerland; arXiv 2510.04280). All use a learned policy as the proposal/nominal sequence for MPPI and sample perturbed rollouts around it. *Delta:* these establish that "learned-prior-as-MPPI-mean + perturbed rollouts" is **standard** — so that mechanic is not novel by itself. Your novelty must come from (a) the prior being a generative WAM imagination seeded from the sim config, and (b) the target being a contact-rich rigid-body sim with the sim as reward/arbiter.
- **PDDM** (deep dynamics models + online MPC for dexterous manipulation) and **Dyna/MBPO** (branched short synthetic rollouts) and **Dreamer/DreamerV3** (learning in imagination): canonical MBRL foundations to cite as the lineage of "learned model accelerates RL," all differing in that the learned model supplies the reward/rollouts rather than the sim being ground truth.

### Tier E — Demonstration/prior-guided & residual RL (overlaps component 2/4; differs on 1, 5)

- **DAPG** (Rajeswaran, Kumar, Gupta, Vezzani, Schulman, Todorov, Levine; RSS 2018; arXiv 1709.10087). Behavior-cloning init + trajectory-tracking auxiliary loss to guide exploration for a **24-DoF ADROIT hand in MuJoCo**; the authors report that "model-free DRL can effectively scale up to complex manipulation tasks with a high-dimensional 24-DoF hand, and solve them from scratch in simulated experiments." *This is the canonical "prior shapes RL exploration, true sim gives return" precedent* — the closest classical analogue to your component-3 philosophy, but the prior is human demos, not a WAM imagination, and there is no realizability benchmark.
- Residual policy learning, AWAC, offline-to-online, imitation-bootstrapped RL (ConRFT), and demonstration-augmented MBRL (multi-stage reward+policy+world-model learning, arXiv 2503.01837) round out the "prior-guided RL" lineage. Goal-Conditioned RL with Imagined Subgoals (Chane-Sane et al., ICML 2021) and "Learning World Models for Unconstrained Goal Navigation" (MUN, arXiv 2411.02446) cover model-generated subgoals.

### Tier F — Realizability / executability / physical-plausibility evaluation (component 5 — the white space)

- **EVA: Aligning Video World Models with Executable Robot Actions via Inverse Dynamics Rewards** (Ruixiang Wang, Qingming Liu, Yueci Deng, Guiliang Liu, Zhen Liu, Kui Jia; CUHK-Shenzhen + DexForce; arXiv 2603.17808, Mar 2026). **Coins the "executability gap,"** defined verbatim: "visually coherent rollouts may still violate rigid-body and kinematic consistency, producing unstable or infeasible control commands when decoded by an IDM. We refer to this mismatch between visual generation and physically executable control as the executability gap." Uses an IDM trained on real robot data as a **reward model** (penalizing velocity/accel/jerk and out-of-bound actions) to RL-align the video model; evaluated on the RoboTwin benchmark and a real bimanual robot. *This is your single closest work on component 5.* *Delta:* arbiter is a **learned IDM**, not a physics simulator; output is a **soft reward**, not a feasibility/infeasibility certificate; mentions rejection sampling only as an inefficient inference-time alternative; not dexterous contact RL.
- **World Action Verifier (WAV)** (Yuejiang Liu, Fan Feng, Lingjing Kong, Weifeng Lu, Jinzhou Tang, Kun Zhang, Kevin Murphy, Chelsea Finn, Yilun Du; arXiv 2604.01985, ICLR 2026 Workshop). Decomposes prediction into **state plausibility** and **action reachability** and verifies each via a sparse inverse model + cycle-consistency. *Closest conceptual neighbor to "feasibility certificate."* *Delta:* the verifier is a **learned** world model + learned sparse inverse model; "reachability" is w.r.t. the learned model, not a physics sim; no interpenetration/energy checks.
- **GVP-WM: Grounding Generated Videos in Feasible Plans via World Models** (arXiv 2602.01960). Projects video plans onto the manifold of dynamically feasible latent trajectories via video-guided latent collocation. *Delta:* feasibility w.r.t. a **learned** model (a reviewer flagged exactly this); it corrects rather than certifies.
- **VP² — A Control-Centric Benchmark for Video Prediction** (Tian, Finn, Wu; Stanford; ICLR 2023; arXiv 2304.13723). Uses a **simulator** to evaluate video-prediction models via **downstream sampling-based-planning task success across 11 task categories and 310 task instance definitions in two simulated environments (a robosuite tabletop-pushing setting and the RoboDesk manipulation environment), with 5000 noisy expert demonstration trajectories per task**. *Closest "simulator-in-the-benchmark" precedent.* *Delta:* measures whether the model is useful for planning (task success), **not** whether a predicted trajectory is itself realizable by some action sequence; no feasibility/infeasibility certificate, no reachable-set framing.
- **Deterministic World Models for Verification of Closed-loop Vision-based Systems** (arXiv 2512.08991). The only paper combining "reachable set" + world model + verification — uses a learned deterministic world model surrogate with conformal-inflated reachable sets to verify **controller safety** (CartPole, Pendulum, etc.). *Delta:* verifies a controller's safety, not whether a generative model's predicted future is physically realizable.
- **"Is the Future Compatible? Diagnosing Dynamic Consistency in World Action Models"** (arXiv 2605.07514). Identifies action-state consistency as a missing reliability axis; diagnostic only, IDM-based, no simulator arbiter, no certificates.
- **Plausibility benchmarks to contrast against (all VLM/human/learned-metric based, none simulator-as-arbiter):** VideoPhy/VideoPhy-2, PhyGenBench/PhyGenEval (arXiv 2410.05363), PhyWorldBench (arXiv 2507.13428, includes an "Anti-Physics" category), Physics-IQ, PhysVidBench, Morpheus (conservation-law metrics on real videos), PhyCoBench (optical-flow + human ranking), Physion. DreamGen Bench correlates generation quality with downstream success. None search a physics sim for a realizing action sequence.

## Synthesis

**(1) Is the exact combination published?** No. The exact stack — WAM seeded from a physics-sim config → used as the sampling-prior mean for MPPI-style perturbed rollouts in that same contact-rich sim → true sim as both reward and feasibility arbiter → dexterous manipulation → realizability-as-benchmark — does not appear in any paper found. The field has bifurcated into (a) "RL inside a *learned* simulator with VLM/learned reward" (WMPO, World-Gymnast, World-Env, VLA-RFT, WoVR, World-VLA-Loop, World4RL, DiWA) and (b) "WAM as a *policy* or as *offline data*" (DreamZero, GR00T N2, DreamGen). Neither keeps the physics sim as ground-truth reward while using the WAM purely to accelerate exploration, and none introduces a simulator-as-reachability-arbiter benchmark.

**(2) Closest single paper and the delta.** **EVA (arXiv 2603.17808)** on the benchmark/feasibility axis (it owns "executability gap" but uses a learned IDM reward, not a sim, and emits no certificate), and **DexWM (arXiv 2512.13644)** on the dexterous-WM-for-planning axis (latent WM + CEM/MPC, but no RL acceleration, no sim reward, no certificate). On the "RL accelerated by imagination" axis, **WMPO** is closest in mechanism but inverts your component 3 (RL is inside the WM with learned reward). The combined delta you own: simulator-as-ground-truth (not learned reward) + WAM-imagination-as-MPPI-prior (not policy-net prior, not offline data) + feasibility-certificate benchmark (not VLM/human plausibility, not downstream-task-success).

**(3) Covered vs. open.**
- **Well-covered (treat as non-novel):** RL in a learned world model (Tier A); learned-prior-as-MPPI-proposal with perturbed rollouts (Tier D); demonstration/prior-guided RL shaping exploration with true return (Tier E, esp. DAPG); WAM joint video+action prediction (Tier B).
- **Open / unclaimed:** (i) the **physics-simulator-as-reachability-arbiter benchmark** with explicit feasibility/infeasibility certificates and physics-violation detection (interpenetration, teleportation, non-conservation) framed as reachable-set non-membership — no paper does this; (ii) **contact-physics-aware feasibility filtering of WAM imaginations** before/inside RL (closest gestures are EVA's IDM reward and GVP-WM's learned-manifold projection, both learned, neither a sim); (iii) seeding the WAM from the sim's actual config so imagination and sim share an identical initial condition for cross-checking.

**(4) Verdict on defensibility.** The direction is **defensible and timely**, but the framing must be surgical because the surrounding cluster is crowded and moving fast (multiple Feb–Apr 2026 preprints). The two strongest, most defensible novel contributions are: (a) **inverting the reward assumption** — physics sim as ground truth, WAM only as exploration accelerant, so WM error degrades efficiency not correctness (a control-variate / variance-reduction framing of policy gradients using true rollouts is a rigorous way to state this); and (b) the **simulator-as-reachability-arbiter benchmark** with hard feasibility/infeasibility certificates for contact-rich dexterous predictions — genuine white space that no current benchmark (VideoPhy, PhyWorldBench, Physics-IQ, DreamGen Bench, VP²) occupies. The "WAM-imagination-as-MPPI-prior" mechanic alone is **not** sufficiently novel (Tier D) and should be presented as engineering, not the core claim. Position explicitly against EVA (learned IDM reward vs. your sim arbiter + certificate), WAV (learned reachability vs. your sim reachable set), VP² (downstream task success vs. your per-trajectory realizability), and DexWM (CEM planning vs. your sim-grounded RL acceleration).

## Recommendations

1. **Lead with the benchmark, not the algorithm.** The simulator-as-reachability-arbiter / feasibility-certificate idea is your clearest white space. Define a feasibility certificate operationally: given a WAM-predicted ~1s trajectory, run a bounded action search (MPPI/CEM) in the contact-rich sim; if a realizing action sequence is found within tolerance, issue a feasibility certificate; else, run physics-violation detectors (interpenetration depth, instantaneous teleportation, kinetic-energy gain without applied work) to issue an infeasibility certificate. Benchmark threshold: report fraction of certified-feasible predictions and correlate with downstream RL acceleration.

2. **State the RL contribution as variance reduction with a correctness guarantee.** Frame the WAM imagination as a proposal that biases exploration while the sim provides unbiased return — i.e., WM error cannot corrupt the optimum, only sample efficiency. This directly differentiates you from WMPO/World-Gymnast/World-Env/VLA-RFT (whose learned reward *can* be hacked). Benchmark threshold that would change the story: if sim-grounded reward gives <1.5x sample-efficiency gain over plain PPO when seeded by the WAM, the prior is not pulling its weight; if WMPO-style in-imagination RL matches your real-robot/sim transfer at lower cost, your correctness argument weakens.

3. **Run the head-to-head against EVA and DexWM explicitly.** Reproduce EVA's IDM-reward alignment and show your sim-arbiter catches infeasibilities the IDM reward misses (especially contact interpenetration and energy violations). Against DexWM, show sim-grounded RL acceleration beats CEM-only planning on the same Franka+Allegro contact-rich tasks.

4. **Seed-from-config ablation.** Demonstrate the value of seeding the WAM from the simulator's actual config (shared initial condition) vs. a free-running WAM, measuring how much tighter the feasibility search becomes. This is a small but genuinely under-explored knob.

5. **Stage the claims.** Submit the benchmark as the primary contribution (white space, low contention) and the sim-grounded WAM-prior RL as the application that the benchmark validates. If a May–June 2026 preprint lands on the exact sim-arbiter framing (do a final arXiv listings sweep before submission), pivot emphasis to the dexterous contact-rich instantiation + the control-variate correctness guarantee, which remains differentiated.

## Caveats
- Several cited works are very recent 2026 preprints (arXiv IDs 2602.xxxxx–2605.xxxxx) consistent with the June 3, 2026 date; some are workshop papers (WAV) or product previews (GR00T N2) rather than peer-reviewed full papers — weight accordingly.
- "RehearseVLA" appears to be a non-existent / placeholder name; World-Env (2509.24948) is its near-exact real referent. World-Gymnast author attribution appears as "Sharma et al." on the arXiv PDF but is cited elsewhere as "Quevedo et al." in the WM survey — verify before citing.
- World-Env's "physically-consistent" claim was softened to "video-based" between arXiv versions; do not over-rely on the "physically-consistent" wording.
- Novelty assessments are bounded by the searches performed; the field is moving weekly, so a final targeted arXiv sweep (May–June 2026) is advised before asserting absolute novelty of the feasibility-certificate benchmark.
- I could not separately verify a peer-reviewed venue for several 2026 preprints; treat venue/year as "arXiv preprint" unless confirmed.
